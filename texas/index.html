How it works

  1. We review your property at no cost

    Send us the address or your notice. We compare the district's value to recent sales of similar property, to the appraised values of comparable properties nearby, and to the income the property actually produces. You get a one-page memo saying whether a protest is worth filing and what we'd argue. If the district is already fair, we tell you that too.

  2. We file and build the case

    One signature on the appointment-of-agent form authorizes us. We file your protest with the appraisal district before the deadline, request the evidence the district plans to use, which Texas law entitles you to see before the hearing, and take the informal meeting with the appraiser. If the district offers a settlement, we tell you whether to take it.

  3. We argue it, and you keep most of the savings

    We present your case to the appraisal review board. If the board gets it wrong, you have 60 days to go further, and we tell you whether binding arbitration or a district court appeal is worth it. When the value comes down, we invoice a share of the first year's savings. The rest is yours.

Office, retail, industrial, warehouse, multifamily, self-storage, hospitality, medical. If you own one building or fifteen and nobody on your team has the protest calendar as their job, that is who we built this for. Larger owners already have a national firm. You should have someone too.

Developers and land holders

Appraisal districts tend to value unsold lots at retail price times lot count, and buildings under construction as if they were finished. Neither is how a buyer would price them. We argue absorption, carrying cost and percent complete, and the lower value becomes the district's starting point next year.

Why equal and uniform matters

Texas gives you a second way to win. Under the Tax Code you can protest not only that your value is above market, but that it is higher than the median appraised value of a reasonable number of comparable properties, appropriately adjusted. The district can have your building about right and still have your neighbors low. That is a winning protest, and it is the argument national firms make for their largest clients. We make it for yours.

Through tax year 2026, Texas also caps annual increases in appraised value at 20 percent for most non-homestead real property valued at about five million dollars or less. The cap is set to expire after 2026 unless the Legislature extends it. We check that the district applied it correctly to your property while it lasts.

Questions owners ask

Can a protest make my value go up?

In principle, yes: the appraisal review board sets the value the evidence supports, and that can be higher than the notice, though it is rare. That is exactly why we review before we file and decline properties where the district is already fair or low. We don't file a case we don't expect to win.

Do I need a lawyer?

Not for the protest. Texas lets a registered property tax consultant represent you with the appraisal district, before the appraisal review board, and in binding arbitration, which is where almost every commercial protest is resolved. If a case is worth taking to district court, we refer it to counsel we work with.

What do you charge?

A percentage of the tax you save in the first year after the protest, invoiced when your reduced bill is confirmed. Nothing up front, and nothing if the value doesn't come down. The percentage is set in your engagement letter before we file.

I already got my notice. Is it too late?

You have until May 15, or 30 days after the notice was delivered if that is later. Send it to us today and we will tell you the deadline and whether there is enough time. If the window has closed, Texas allows a late correction for some substantial errors, and otherwise we put your property on our list for next year's notice.

Does a win freeze my value like it does in Georgia?

No. Texas does not freeze a protested value, which is why most commercial owners protest every year it makes sense. A lower value this year is the base the district starts from next year, and we keep the file open so the next notice gets the same review.

What do you need from me?

For the review, just the address. If you engage us, one signature on the appointment-of-agent form, and for income-producing property a rent roll and the last twelve months of operating statements help us build the strongest case. We handle the rest.
